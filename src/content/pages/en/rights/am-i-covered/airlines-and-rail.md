**If you work for an airline or a railroad, the Railway Labor Act — not the NLRA — is your law, and the National Mediation Board — not the NLRB — is your agency.**

## What this means for you

The Railway Labor Act (RLA) covers airlines and railroads. The rules differ from the NLRA in ways that matter:

- **Filing for an election needs a showing of interest from 50% of the craft or class** — not 30% like the NLRA [A42].
- **The election is decided by a majority of valid ballots cast** [A43].
- There's a mandatory mediation step with the National Mediation Board (NMB) before a "release" countdown to self-help (strikes) can begin. The process is slower and more formal than the NLRA.

## What to do now

1. Aim for a big majority — well past 50% — before you file anything.
2. Contact the union that organizes your craft: flight attendants (AFA-CWA), pilots (ALPA), mechanics and ramp (IAM or Teamsters), or [get routed](/unions).
3. Talk to an organizer who knows RLA campaigns — the timeline and tactics differ from NLRA drives.
