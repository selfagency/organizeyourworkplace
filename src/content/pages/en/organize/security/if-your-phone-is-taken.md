**A seized or lost phone is the worst-case. Prepare for it before it happens.**

## What this means for you

If your phone is lost, seized at a border, or grabbed during a workplace incident, everything on it is exposed — unless you prepared.

**Before anything happens:**

1. **Encrypt and lock.** Full-disk encryption is on by default on modern phones. Use a 6+ digit passcode or alphanumeric — not your fingerprint alone. (In the US, biometrics have weaker legal protection than passcodes in some situations; a passcode you can refuse to give.)
2. **Signal: screen lock on.** Signal requires your biometrics to open. Disappearing messages shrink what's there to find.
3. **The workspace app (R3):** app lock with a separate passcode, auto-lock, and a **wipe button** that also clears unsent updates.
4. **Disappearing messages everywhere.** Old messages that no longer exist can't be read.
5. **One-time mailbox keys** (workspace): old encrypted updates become unreadable when keys rotate.

## If it happens now

1. If you can, remotely wipe the device (Find My iPhone / Find My Device) — from *someone else's* phone, not yours.
2. Tell your committee immediately: they should assume the phone's contents are compromised and rotate what they can.
3. Say nothing to whoever holds the phone without talking to a lawyer first.
