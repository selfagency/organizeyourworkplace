**Three to seven trusted coworkers, meeting regularly, each owning a piece of the workplace.**

## What this means for you

The committee is the engine of the campaign. One person can talk to dozens; a committee can reach everyone, every week.

- **Size:** 3–7 for most workplaces. Big enough to cover every department and shift. Small enough to meet in a car.
- **The core:** the 3–7 *most trusted* members. They hold the wall chart, the plan, and the keys.
- **Jobs:** each member owns a list of coworkers — they have the one-on-ones, they update the chart, they bring problems back to the committee.

## What to do now

1. From your [leaders](/organize/leaders) list, invite the strongest 2–3 to a first meeting — somewhere private, off work property.
2. Agree on the problem you're solving and the plan to solve it.
3. Split the workplace: each member takes a department or shift.
4. Meet weekly. Same day, same time, short.

## Watch out

Committee members are the first people the boss will target when organizing surfaces. Before you go public, every member should know [what the boss can and can't do](/rights/tips) and have their [security basics](/organize/security) down.
