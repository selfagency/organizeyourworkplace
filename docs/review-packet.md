## /contract/strikes (en)

- file: `en/contract/strikes.md`
- title: Strikes: what kind, what rights, what it costs
- status as of: 2026-10-26
- review: pending
- sources cited: A80

Claim-bearing lines:

> Federal law sorts strikers into two classes, and the difference is money and jobs [A80]:
> Economic strikers** strike to win better wages or conditions from a lawful employer. You keep your employee status and can't be fired for striking — but the employer may **permanently replace** you, and the job goes to the replacement [A80].
> Unfair labor practice (ULP) strikers** strike over the employer's law violations — refusing to bargain, firing union leaders, surveillance. ULP strikers have **greater reinstatement rights**: the employer must reinstate them when the strike ends, even if that means displacing replacements [A80].
> What's not protected:** sit-down strikes, slowdowns, intermittent strikes, and strikes that break a no-strike clause in an existing contract can get you fired [A80]. So can picket-line violence — strike misconduct costs legal protection [A80].
> 5. **Make unconditional offers to return** if the strike stalls — it preserves your reinstatement rights [A80].

> Plazos:** elecciones al menos cada **5 años** en nacionales/internacionales, **4 años** en organismos intermedios, **3 años** en locales [A82].

> Sin dinero del sindicato ni del empleador para campañas.** Los fondos del sindicato solo pagan los costos de realizar la elección [A83].
> Registros conservados un año**; miembros que agotan los recursos internos (o no obtienen decisión en 3 meses) pueden quejarse ante la Secretaría del Trabajo dentro del mes siguiente [A83].
