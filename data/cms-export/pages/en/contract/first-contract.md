**First contracts take longer than any that follow. The employer's game is delay. The counter is momentum.**

## What this means for you

After certification, federal law requires the employer to bargain in good faith — but "good faith" does not mean "agree." Employers drag first contracts by showing up unprepared, cancelling sessions, demanding mountains of information, and stalling every article. Starbucks won election after election starting in 2021 while bargaining dragged for years — nearly 700 stores organized and still without a first contract [P1-11].

What actually gets contracts signed: workers visibly ready to apply pressure. Every signed first contract came with a committee that kept acting between sessions.

## What to do now

1. **Bargain during the campaign, not after.** Run the [survey](/contract/survey) before the election so demands are ready in week one.
2. **Set a contract deadline.** Open bargaining with a public date: "we want a contract by March 1." Deadlines create pressure.
3. **Escalate on schedule.** See the [escalation ladder](/contract/campaign): petitions, button days, picket support, practice strikes, then the real thing.
4. **Bargain in public.** Contract updates to every member after every session. Secrecy is where campaigns die.
5. **Use what's public.** Contracts in your industry are searchable in OLMS's e-CBA file [A81] — see [compare contracts](/contract/compare) for what's realistic.

## Watch out

Don't file a failure-to-bargain charge for every slow week — the NLRB moves slower than your deadline and bad-faith claims are hard to prove for mere stalling [P1-11]. Charges are for real refusals (see [file a charge](/help/file-a-charge)). Your leverage is your members, not the paperwork.
