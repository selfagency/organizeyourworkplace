**Union contracts are public records. Before you write your demands, read what unions like yours actually won.**

## What this means for you

The Department of Labor keeps a public file of collective bargaining agreements — the e-CBA — collected since 1947 [A81]. It's searchable by industry, employer, and union. When the boss says "$22 is crazy," you can put a real contract from a real shop in your sector on the table and say "they got it."

The comparison tool in this section searches the same file for your industry and pulls wage scales, scheduling clauses, and first-contract examples.

## What to do now

1. Open the [contract compare tool](/toolkit/contract-compare) — it runs locally and searches public OLMS data [A81].
2. Look for **contracts with employers like yours** (size, sector, region) — a Kroger contract in Ohio beats a Google myth for a grocery drive.
3. Copy the **structure**, not just the numbers: notice periods, step raises, grievance timelines. Good language is reusable.
4. Bring printouts to bargaining. "Exhibit A: what your competitor pays under a union contract" ends more debates than any speech.

## Watch out

Old contracts describe old economies — check the year before citing a 2009 wage scale. And contracts only cover the shops that file them; gaps in the data aren't proof something doesn't exist. When the file comes up empty, ask unions in your sector directly — see [find a union](/unions).
