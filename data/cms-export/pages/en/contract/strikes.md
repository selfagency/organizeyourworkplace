**Not all strikes are the same. Which kind you're on determines whether you can be permanently replaced — so know before you walk.**

## What this means for you

Federal law sorts strikers into two classes, and the difference is money and jobs [A80]:

- **Economic strikers** strike to win better wages or conditions from a lawful employer. You keep your employee status and can't be fired for striking — but the employer may **permanently replace** you, and the job goes to the replacement [A80].
- **Unfair labor practice (ULP) strikers** strike over the employer's law violations — refusing to bargain, firing union leaders, surveillance. ULP strikers have **greater reinstatement rights**: the employer must reinstate them when the strike ends, even if that means displacing replacements [A80].

In practice, most first-contract strikes mix both — workers demanding a contract from an employer that's also committing violations. Keep a record of every violation during your campaign; it matters later. See [file a charge](/help/file-a-charge).

**What a strike costs:** no paychecks (that's what strike funds are for — one more reason a union affiliation matters, see [going independent](/unions/independent)); health coverage risk; real strain on members. Strikes are won by funds, food pantries, and solidarity — not slogans.

**What's not protected:** sit-down strikes, slowdowns, intermittent strikes, and strikes that break a no-strike clause in an existing contract can get you fired [A80]. So can picket-line violence — strike misconduct costs legal protection [A80].

## What to do now

1. **Never strike without a vote.** Supermajority, in person, with the costs written down. See [the escalation ladder](/contract/campaign).
2. **Build the strike fund math first:** how many weeks can members survive? Food bank, hardship fund, ally support — set it up before the vote, not after.
3. **Demand your organizer's playbook:** picket captains per shift, daily rallies, press plan, what to do when the boss sends replacements or threatens people.
4. **Set an end-game before you start:** what gets you back to work? A contract number, a binding card-check, a ULP settlement? Open-ended strikes end badly.
5. **Make unconditional offers to return** if the strike stalls — it preserves your reinstatement rights [A80].

## Watch out

This page is the map, not the terrain. Strike strategy has more legal traps than any other part of organizing — involve your union and check [what's changed](/rights/whats-changed). Never strike to protest the boss being a bad person; strike for a demand the employer can concede.
