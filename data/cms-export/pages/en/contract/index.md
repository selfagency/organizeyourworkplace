**Winning the election isn't the finish line. It's the starting line of the second campaign: the contract.**

## What this means for you

Nearly 700 Starbucks stores have won elections and still have no contract [P1-11]. The company simply refuses to bargain seriously, and months turn into years. That's not bad luck — it's strategy. Employers know a certified union without a contract has power on paper only.

A contract is what turns your union from a legal status into a working reality: raises in writing, a grievance procedure, just-cause protection, schedules you can plan a life around.

## What to do now

1. Start before the election if you can — [run the bargaining survey](/contract/survey) early so you can hit the ground running.
2. Read [why first contracts take forever](/contract/first-contract) and how to speed them up.
3. Build the [bargaining committee](/contract/bargaining-committee) and turn survey results into [demands](/contract/demands).
4. Plan the [escalation campaign](/contract/campaign) that backs the table up — from buttons to strikes.
5. Learn [strike basics](/contract/strikes) before you need them, and [compare real contracts](/contract/compare) from your industry.

## Watch out

The boss's playbook doesn't end at the election — it changes shape. Expect "we can't afford it" at the table and delays everywhere. The counter is the same as always: an organized, visible, united membership.
