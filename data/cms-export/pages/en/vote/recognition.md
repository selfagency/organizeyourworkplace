**Show the boss a majority, and he has two weeks: recognize you, or file for an election himself. That's the Cemex rule.**

## What this means for you

In August 2023 the NLRB's Cemex decision (372 NLRB No. 130) set a new sequence [A74]:

1. Your union **requests recognition**, showing that a majority of employees in the unit have designated the union as their representative.
2. The employer must then either **recognize and bargain**, or **file an election petition** with the NLRB within two weeks.
3. If the boss refuses both — or breaks labor law during the fight — the NLRB can order the union certified **without any election at all**, and the boss has to bargain.

Why this matters to you: recognition skips the election entirely. No month of captive meetings, no firing of leaders, no campaign attrition. It's the shortest road to the table.

## What to do now

1. Get to majority (aim for ~70% — see [cards](/vote/cards)).
2. With your union, deliver the recognition demand in writing. Keep the majority proof out of the boss's hands unless the strategy calls for it.
3. If the boss stalls past two weeks without filing for an election, that's potentially an unfair labor practice — see [file a charge](/help/file-a-charge).

## Watch out — the law is under threat

Business groups have attacked Cemex in court, and a more employer-friendly NLRB could revisit it. As of the date on this page it stands [A74], but check [what's changed](/rights/whats-changed) and confirm with your union organizer before you count on it. Don't build the whole campaign around one legal case: build a majority so strong it wins either road — recognition or election.
