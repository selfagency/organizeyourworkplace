**Every one of these lines has been field-tested on workers just like you. Here are the answers, short enough to say out loud.**

## The 15 arguments

1. **"Unions are a third party."** The union is us — the workers in this building. The only real third party is the consultant the company is paying to run this campaign.
2. **"We're a family here."** Families don't cut your hours by email. If we're family, we can have a real say — that's all the union is.
3. **"Dues are expensive."** Dues start only after we've voted on and won a contract — and we set them, by vote. Ask the boss what the raises cost us when he announced his last "restructuring."
4. **"The union will make you strike."** Only members vote to strike, and it takes a serious majority. No one can order you out. What the boss *can* do right now — cut your hours — no one votes on.
5. **"You'll lose the benefits you have."** Nothing goes down without our vote. The contract locks in what we have and improves what we can. His promise to "keep things the same" is the only thing here that's written on air.
6. **"Just give me a chance to fix things."** Write it down and sign it, and we'll compare it to the contract. That's the difference between a promise and a right: one is enforced.
7. **"Companies close because of unions."** Companies close because of bad business decisions. Union shops close at rates no worse than anyone else — but union workers leave with severance and notice. That's the part he's not mentioning.
8. **"The union doesn't know your job."** You do — and you'll write the proposals. The union brings negotiators and lawyers; the demands come from this room.
9. **"It'll be us against them."** It already is. The union just makes it a fair fight.
10. **"You'll have to pay fines to the union."** Fines for crossing a picket line are rare, set democratically, and mostly a myth in right-to-work states — where they're unenforceable anyway. He's describing a horror movie he hasn't seen.
11. **"The union protects bad workers."** The contract protects fair process — progressive discipline, real cause. Good workers get protection they've never had: you can't be written up for the shift lead's bad mood.
12. **"We can't promise raises if you unionize."** Correct — and he can't promise raises now either. What a union changes is that we get a seat where those decisions happen.
13. **"You can just vote it out later if you don't like it."** True — and that's why this vote is low-risk. Decertification exists. What he's really saying: the union has to keep delivering. Agreed.
14. **"Now is not the right time."** It never is. The right time was before the last round of cuts. The second-best time is now.
15. **"Signing a card means you're stuck."** A card is a request for a vote [A68] — see [what cards are](/vote/cards). The vote is secret. He can't check how you voted, and neither can we. That's the law [A68].

## What to do now

1. Rehearse the ones you hear most, out loud, with your committee.
2. When you hear a new one, write it down and bring it back — this list grows in real drives.
3. Any argument that comes with a **threat or a promise attached** is illegal — log it and see [file a charge](/help/file-a-charge).
