**A card is a request, not a vote, and not a contract. Know exactly what it says before you hand it to anyone.**

## What this means for you

An authorization card says, in essence: *this worker wants this union to represent them for bargaining with this employer.* That's all. Signing does not:

- pay dues (dues start after a first contract, if the contract says so),
- force you to strike,
- or cast a vote — the election is the vote.

What cards actually do:

- Give the union proof of support to **ask the boss for recognition** — and since the 2023 Cemex decision, if a majority of the unit has designated the union, the boss must recognize it or file for an election within two weeks [A74].
- Serve as the **showing of interest** for an NLRB election petition — at least 30% of the unit must support it for the NLRB to even process the petition [A68].
- Tell your committee privately where the support stands, so you know when you're ready.

## What to do now

1. **Read the card before you sign.** It should name your employer and the union. If it doesn't, ask why.
2. **Sign with your personal info, not work systems.** Physical card or a secure digital form — never a work email.
3. **Keep the pipeline private.** The boss doesn't get to see who signed; the NLRB gets copies only when a petition is filed [A69].
4. **Aim high before using them.** 30% is the legal floor [A68]. Organizers aim for around 70% before going public, because the boss's campaign will peel people off.

## Watch out

Cards have expiration — many say they're valid for a set period. If your drive stalls for months, ask your union whether fresh cards are needed.
