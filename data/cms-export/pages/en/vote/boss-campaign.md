**The anti-union campaign is a script. Companies pay consultants six figures to run it. Read the script first and it loses its power.**

## What this means for you

Expect the campaign in phases, roughly in this order:

1. **"The open door."** Suddenly management cares about your problems. A new suggestion box, a promised committee of one, a raise for "top performers."
2. **Supervisor one-on-ones.** Your direct boss asks each worker how they feel about the union. Legal — but the purpose is intel and pressure, delivered by the person who controls your schedule.
3. **Letters and emails.** Patriotic-tuned warnings about "third parties," dues, strikes. Carefully worded to be technically true.
4. **Captive meetings.** Mandatory anti-union sessions, often run by a hired consultant. See [captive meetings](/vote/captive-meetings).
5. **Promises and improvements.** Raises announced mid-campaign. Legal to grant raises; illegal to promise them *for rejecting* the union. Write down which one happened (see [TIPS](/rights/what-the-boss-cant-do)).
6. **Fear escalation.** "Companies close because of unions." Statistically, that's a scare line — but it works on scared people. Inoculate early (see [tell them what's coming](/organize/inoculation)).
7. **Delay and attrition.** Legal challenges, dragged-out hearings, schedule changes that break up pro-union shifts. The point is to outlast you.

Decode it live: the decoder tool in this section (see the toolkit) walks you through any statement your boss makes and shows which move it is.

## What to do now

1. **Inoculate before the campaign starts.** People who hear the script from *you* first hear lies from the boss second [A44].
2. **Document everything.** Letters, meeting times, who attended, exact quotes. It feeds [ULP charges](/help/file-a-charge) and your sanity.
3. **Talk one-on-ones.** The campaign works on isolation. Counter it with conversations — see [how to listen](/organize/listening).

## Watch out

Most of the playbook is legal. That's the point — consultants design campaigns that stay just inside the law. Your protection is preparation and unity, not the NLRB's speed.
