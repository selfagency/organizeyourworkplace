**The NLRB needs 30% support to run an election. You need closer to 70% to win one. Don't confuse the two.**

## What this means for you

Filing an RC petition (Form NLRB-502) with your regional NLRB office starts the formal election process [A69]. The legal requirements:

- **At least 30% of the bargaining unit** must show support for the petition [A68].
- The petition names the unit and the union; the **showing of interest goes to the NLRB, never to the employer** [A69].
- The NLRB investigates: jurisdiction, union qualification, no contract or recent election that would bar a vote [A68].

Then the process runs [A70]:

1. The employer must post a **Notice of Petition for Election** where notices go.
2. If no agreement on terms, a **pre-election hearing** is set — typically about a week out.
3. The employer may file a **statement of position** raising disputes (unit, eligibility, timing).
4. The Regional Director orders an election if a question of representation exists.

## What to do now

1. **Wait for ~70%.** The boss gets the voter list and runs his campaign for the weeks before the vote. 30% files; 70% survives.
2. **File with your union organizer** — they prepare the form and the card evidence. Information officers at the regional office help free of charge [A69].
3. **Expect the boss's disputes.** Unit fights and eligibility challenges are delay tactics. See [the unit](/vote/the-unit).
4. **Keep organizing between filing and voting.** The petition is not a rest period — it's the boss's turn to campaign. Go to [the boss's playbook](/vote/boss-campaign) now.

## Watch out

Filing too early hands the boss a map of your support and weeks to tear it down. There are also timing bars — recent elections or a live contract can block a petition. Your organizer checks these before filing.
