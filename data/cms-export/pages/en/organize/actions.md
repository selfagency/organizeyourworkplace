**You don't need a union to act like one. Small, visible wins build the muscle you'll need for the big one.**

## What this means for you

A drive that only collects cards for six months dies quietly. A drive that fixes the breakroom fridge in week two builds a story people can tell: "When we acted together, we won."

These fights are legal. Acting together to improve your workplace is protected activity — union or no union, no card needed. See [Section 7 rights](/rights/section-7).

## What to do now

Pick a fight that is **winnable, visible, and low-risk**:

1. **Petition:** ten names asking for the AC to be fixed. Signatures are protected activity.
2. **Group complaint:** three coworkers raise the same broken schedule with the same manager, in writing, on the same day.
3. **March on the boss:** the whole shift walks to the office together about one clear issue. Respectful, united, calm — and hard to punish.
4. **Button day or shirt day:** everyone wearing the same thing ("Respect is not a favor") on the same day. Visual unity without a single word.
5. **Win it, then tell it.** After the win, spread the story: "We asked together, they fixed it. Imagine what a contract could do."

Escalate slowly. Each fight should involve slightly more people than the last.

## Watch out

Don't pick a fight you can't win — a loud loss teaches people that acting together gets you nothing. And keep every action about a shared workplace issue, not about personalities. That keeps it protected activity and keeps your coworkers on side.
