**"It won't change anything" usually means "I'm scared it won't." Both are normal. Both are workable.**

## What this means for you

Every organizing drive hits the same three walls:

- **Fear:** "I'll get fired." Rational — bosses do retaliate. The answer is protection in numbers, not pretending the risk is zero.
- **Apathy:** "Whatever." Usually exhaustion, not indifference. The job has already taken everything; another project sounds heavy.
- **Hopelessness:** "We tried in 2019 and it went nowhere." History is the wound. Treat it with respect, not correction.

## What to do now

1. **For fear, show the math.** One person alone can be fired. When 60% of the shop is signed on, firing people means shutting the place down. The boss counts better than anyone — that's why bosses fight unions.
2. **For apathy, shrink the ask.** Not "join the union" — "come to a cookout Saturday." Momentum starts with one shared win: a broken AC fixed, a fired coworker's shift restored.
3. **For hopelessness, name what went wrong.** Usually it was no committee, no map, no plan — not "unions don't work here." Ask: "What would you do differently this time?"
4. **Take a small fight and win it.** A public, low-risk win changes what people believe is possible. See [small fights first](/organize/actions).
5. **Share the odds honestly.** Most drives that get to an election win the vote — but only a fraction of shops that start ever get there. The difference is organization, not luck. See [is it worth it](/start/is-it-worth-it).

## Watch out

Never mock the scared or the cynical. Today's "no" is often next month's organizer — if you left them their dignity.
