**One coworker. Private. Your own time. Listen first.**

## What this means for you

The first conversation is the atom of organizing. Every big campaign is hundreds of these.

**Where:** on breaks, walking out, over text from your personal phone. Never in a work system.

**Lead with problems, not the word "union."** UFCW's organizer guide is blunt about this: don't open with "I'm thinking about starting a union" unless you already trust the person. Ask about hours, pay and safety — the things a contract would protect [P1-6].

**The sequence:**

1. **Listen.** "What's the most messed-up thing about working here?" Then shut up and listen.
2. **Agitate.** Connect their problem to the cause: "Who decides that? Could we?"
3. **Show a plan.** "Some of us are talking about acting together. Here's how that works."
4. **Inoculate.** "The boss will say X. Here's the truth about that."
5. **Ask for one specific commitment.** "Can you talk to [one coworker] this week?" Not "will you join" — one small, concrete next step.
6. **Follow up.** A commitment without a follow-up is a dead commitment.

## What to do now

1. Pick one coworker who's angry about the same thing you are.
2. Have the conversation this week. Use the [conversation builder](/toolkit/conversation-builder) to plan it.
3. Write down what they said (on your own device, never theirs).

## Watch out

Don't argue. Don't lecture. If they push back, you've learned something — note it and move on. The goal of conversation one is not a signature. It's a second conversation.
