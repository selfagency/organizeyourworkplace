**People don't join a union because you argued well. They join because they felt heard.**

## What this means for you

A one-on-one is 80% listening. Your job is not to convince. Your job is to find out what makes this person angry at work — and connect it to the union.

Signs you're talking too much: you're explaining card rules in minute two. Stop. Ask another question.

## What to do now

1. **Ask open questions** and shut up. "What would you change about this place if you could?" Then wait. Silence pulls out the real answer.
2. **Follow the anger.** When they name a problem — the schedule, a manager, pay — dig in: "Tell me more about that. When did it start? Who else is mad about it?"
3. **Agitate, gently.** Ask: "Is that just how it has to be?" Let them arrive at "no" themselves. It sticks better than your "no."
4. **Take mental notes.** Afterward, write down what they said while it's fresh — on your own phone, never a work device. It feeds your [workplace map](/organize/map-your-workplace).
5. **Ask for a small step.** Not a card. Something smaller: "Can I check back Friday?" A yes to anything makes the next yes easier.

## Watch out

Don't promise what you can't deliver. Don't trash the boss by name in writing — see [security](/organize/security). And if someone is scared, don't push. Fear is information: they may need more trust before they need more facts.
