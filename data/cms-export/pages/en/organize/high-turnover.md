**When half the staff turns over every year, organizing one store isn't enough. Organize the industry.**

## What this means for you

Fast food turnover tops 150% a year. Dollar stores, warehouses, call centers — the same. A campaign that takes 12 months can lose half its supporters before the vote.

The answer, proven by the Union of Southern Service Workers: **organize by city and employer, not by store.** USSW organizes cross-employer service workers in NC, SC, GA and AL, using escalating direct action — button days, petition deliveries, walkouts [P1-10].

## What this means for your campaign

1. **Move faster.** Compress the timeline: map, committee, and public action in months, not a year.
2. **Organize the employer, not the location.** Every store of the same chain in your city is one campaign.
3. **Use public action early.** Button days and petition deliveries build identity that survives turnover — workers leave, the campaign stays.
4. **Recruit the new hires.** Every orientation class is a fresh list of one-on-ones.

## What to do now

1. Find out whether a union already organizes your employer across locations — [the matcher](/unions/matcher) knows.
2. If it's a Southern service employer, [USSW](/unions) may already be in your city.
3. If nothing exists, [EWOC](/help/worker-centers) coaches cross-employer campaigns.

## Watch out

Cross-employer campaigns need a union or support organization behind them — the legal structure (one petition per unit) doesn't fit them. Don't try it alone.
