**Data opens ears. Stories open hearts. Your workplace is full of stories nobody has assembled yet — that's committee work.**

## What this means for you

Every shop has them: the veteran who trained three managers and got laid off in the fourth restructure; the single parent losing shifts to schedule games; the injury that everyone knows was the broken machine's fault. Individually they're private gripes. Together they're a case.

## What to do now

1. **Collect in one-on-ones.** The question that works: "Tell me about the moment this place stopped being OK." Write it down afterward, on your own device — see [security](/organize/security).
2. **Get permission for every use.** A story told at a meeting is different from a story on a leaflet. Ask each time, name or no name — never decide for someone.
3. **Shape it: person, moment, stake, fix.** "Maria, the schedule changed the night before her kid's surgery, nobody notified her, the contract's 72-hour rule ends that."
4. **Bank them for the campaign.** Leaflets, captive meetings, testimony to the board — stories are your ammunition and your inoculation, see [tell them what's coming](/organize/inoculation).
5. **Tell your own when it helps.** A committee member's story told plainly does more than a perfect stat sheet.

## Watch out

Don't exaggerate — one caught embellishment poisons every story after it. And don't turn coworkers into exhibits: someone's worst week is not your content to share without their yes.
