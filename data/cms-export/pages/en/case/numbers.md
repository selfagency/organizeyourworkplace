**Here's what unions measurably change — with the honest caveats included, because overselling this costs you credibility later.**

## The headline numbers (BLS, 2025) [A85]

- **10.0%** of US wage and salary workers are union members — **14.7 million** people.
- **Nonunion median weekly earnings are 84% of union earnings:** $1,174 vs **$1,404**.
- Unionization by sector: **5.9% private**, **32.9% public** (more than five times higher).
- Highest rates: education, training and library occupations **32.5%**; protective service **31.3%**; local government **37.8%**.

The earnings gap is raw, not adjusted for job, experience, or region — controlled studies put the union wage premium somewhat smaller but real and largest for lower-wage workers [P1-12]. The honest line: "unions raise pay — most for the people lowest on the scale."

## Why the private-sector rate is 5.9% [A85]

Not because unions stopped working — because union density fell after decades of opposition, and because most workers never get the chance: employers fire pro-union workers illegally, and penalties arrive years too late to matter. That's exactly why this site exists: the rules only work for workers who know them.

## What to do now

1. **Use numbers sparingly and locally.** "$1,404 versus $1,174" lands. "See table 3" doesn't.
2. **Compare contracts, not averages:** the [contract compare tool](/contract/compare) shows what your industry's actual union contracts pay.
3. **Bring the caveat yourself** before a skeptic does — "raw comparison, not a controlled study" buys you the next five minutes of listening.

## Watch out

Never claim unions fix everything. Union members still get tired, hurt, and underpaid compared to what they want — they just have a contract, a process, and a vote. Promise that, not paradise.

Sources: BLS Union Members — 2025 [A85]; EPI union wage analysis [P1-12].
