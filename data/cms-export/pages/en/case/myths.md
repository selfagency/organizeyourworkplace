**The same myths circulate in every breakroom in the country. Here they are, with what's true inside each one.**

## "Unions are corrupt."

Some were — and members ousted them, because union members can vote out leadership and the law forces open books (public LM financial reports [A84]; fair elections [A82]). Your local's corruption risk is controlled by your own bylaws and elections. Compare that to your employer, whose books you'll never see.

## "Unions just want your dues."

Dues typically start only after a first contract — and are set by member vote. A union that collects dues from unhappy members isn't viable; a contract that raises pay is. The $1,404 vs $1,174 weekly gap [A85] is what the dues buy.

## "Unions protect lazy workers."

Contracts protect **process**: progressive discipline, real cause, a chance to respond. Good workers get protection they've never had — from favoritism, from budgets, from a supervisor's bad week. The lazy-worker story is how the boss recruits you to police your coworkers instead of the schedule.

## "We don't need one — it's a good place to work."

Then a union costs you little and locks in the good parts. "Good place" without a contract is a compliment the boss can revoke. Every benefit you have is one bad quarter away from review.

## "A union will make everything a fight."

Everything is already a negotiation — you just negotiate alone, with no leverage. The union makes the negotiations formal and winnable.

## "It's too late / too small / not worth it."

Two percent of a small shop can win small fights immediately [P1-6]. The question isn't the size of your shop — it's whether you're willing to talk to the people in it. Start at [the first conversation](/organize/first-conversation).

## Watch out

Don't recite these as a list. Answer the myth your coworker actually said, in their language, with the [numbers](/case/numbers) where they help. For the boss's version of these arguments — with the campaign pressure attached — see [answers to the top 15](/vote/answers).
