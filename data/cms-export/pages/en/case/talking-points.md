**Short answers to the questions coworkers actually ask, by issue. Say them in your own words — these are the skeleton.**

## Pay

- "A union doesn't set your raise — we do, in a contract the boss has to sign. Right now his word is the whole contract."
- "Union members' median weekly pay is $1,404 versus $1,174 nonunion [A85]. Not magic — it's what happens when pay is written down."

## Safety

- "One person reporting a hazard is a complainer. Fifty reporting it together is a legal protected activity — and OSHA takes it seriously."
- "The contract puts safety language in writing, so 'we'll look into it' has a deadline."

## Scheduling

- "A contract can require advance notice of schedules and pay for changes. Your current 'policy' can change Monday because it's written on air."
- "Imagine planning childcare without checking your phone every night. That's what scheduling clauses are."

## Respect

- "Discipline at will means discipline by mood. Just cause means a warning ladder, evidence, and a chance to tell your side."
- "Respect isn't a poster in the breakroom. It's the power to say 'no, that's not in the contract.'"

## "Why now?"

- "The best time was before the last round of cuts. The second-best time is now — the boss's next 'restructuring' is already scheduled."
- "It's not that things are worst now. It's that the union is how things ever got better."

## What to do now

1. Pick the two issues that dominate your shop (from your [workplace map](/organize/map-your-workplace)) and memorize those talking points.
2. Practice out loud with your committee — reading is not saying.
3. When a coworker gives you a better line, add it. This page is a starter kit, not scripture.

## Watch out

Talking points are for answering questions, not for opening conversations. The opener is always their anger, not your data — see [the first conversation](/organize/first-conversation).
