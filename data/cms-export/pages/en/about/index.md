**This site makes claims about your rights and your money. Here's exactly how we make them.**

## Sources and methods

- **Every factual claim is cited** to a source ID (like [A85]) that resolves in our public source list. Legal claims come from primary sources — the NLRB, the Department of Labor/OLMS, BLS, the Federal Register. How-to content comes from organizer practice — Labor Notes, EWOC, union training material.
- **Legal pages carry a "status as of" date** and link to the [legal changelog](/rights/whats-changed). Labor law moves; a page without a date is a liability, so every legal page has one.
- **Union contact links appear only when verified** against the union's own website. Where we couldn't verify an intake link, we say so and route you to organizations that cover everyone ([EWOC](/unions/no-union-yet), the AFL-CIO).
- **We mark what we don't know.** Unverified claims are held back with a TODO marker, not published. When a fact can't be confirmed, the page says so.
- **This is not legal advice.** See the notice on every legal page, and [lawyers and worker centers](/help/lawyers) when you need advice about your situation.

## Source tiers

1. **Primary:** NLRB, BLS, OLMS, court opinions — legal claims and statistics.
2. **Labor research:** EPI, UC Berkeley Labor Center, Cornell ILR — the economic case.
3. **Organizer practice:** Labor Notes, EWOC, union guides — how-to content.
4. **Management-side law firms:** used only to track legal changes, never cited as advice, and always confirmed against a primary source.

## Who runs this

The site is run by its founder with the goal of handing it to EWOC or a union. It takes no money from employers or unions for listings, runs no ads, and lists unions neutrally, ranked by verified organizing activity — not opinion.
