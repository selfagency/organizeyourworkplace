**You're interviewing them as much as they're interviewing you. Ask these 10.**

## The 10 questions

1. **Who organizes workers like us, and where?** Ask for campaigns in your industry in the last 2 years — names, outcomes.
2. **Who would run our campaign — us or you?** The best answer: "You do, with our support." A union that runs everything from the top builds a weak committee.
3. **What resources do you commit?** Organizer time, money for mailers, legal support. Get specifics.
4. **What's your strike fund like?** If the campaign needs teeth, who pays members during a strike?
5. **How do you handle the boss's retaliation?** ULP charges, public pressure, legal support — what's the playbook?
6. **What do dues cost, and when do they start?** Usually after the first contract. Get the number.
7. **What happens after we win?** Contract bargaining support, steward training, staff assigned to us.
8. **Who decides our demands?** The members should. Watch for answers that say otherwise.
9. **Can we talk to a committee you've organized recently?** A union confident in its work says yes.
10. **What do you need from us?** The honest answer is usually: one-on-ones, a committee, and patience.

## What this means for you

Different unions have different styles — some are staff-driven, some member-driven. Neither is wrong, but the fit matters. A committee that wants to lead should pick a union that lets it.

## What to do now

1. Call two or three unions from the [profiles](/unions/profiles).
2. Ask all 10. Write down the answers.
3. Compare. Pick the one whose answers you believe.

## Watch out

Don't pick by name recognition. A famous union with no organizer capacity in your region is worse than a smaller one that shows up.
