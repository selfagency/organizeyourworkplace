**You call. They ask questions. Here's the script, both sides.**

## What this means for you

When you call a union's organizing line, expect:

1. **Screening questions:** your employer, your industry, how many workers, what's the problem, have you talked to coworkers yet.
2. **A confidentiality talk:** organizers won't act without your say-so. Nothing moves until you're ready.
3. **An organizer assigned:** usually a volunteer (EWOC) or staff organizer who calls you back within days.
4. **The first real meeting:** they'll want a one-on-one with you, then — only when you're ready — with coworkers you trust.

**What they'll ask you to do:** keep talking to coworkers, one at a time. Not sign anything, not announce anything. The union can't organize your workplace; only you and your coworkers can. The union brings resources, experience, and the legal machinery.

**Use a personal phone and personal email.** Say so on the call if the form asks for work contacts — organizers know the drill.

## What to do now

1. Pick a union from the [profiles](/unions/profiles) and call.
2. Before the call, write down: employer name, headcount, the top 3 problems, how many coworkers you've talked to.
3. After the call, keep organizing — the union moves at your pace, not the other way.

## Watch out

If a union pushes you to go public before you have a committee and a majority, that's a red flag. See [how to pick](/unions/how-to-pick).
