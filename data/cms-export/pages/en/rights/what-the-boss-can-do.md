**The boss can share facts, opinions, and examples — no threats. Remember it as FOE.**

## What this means for you

Employer anti-union speech is legal as long as it stays on this side of the line:

- **F — Facts.** "Union dues at our size would be about $X a month." Real numbers, no threats.
- **O — Opinions.** "I don't think a union would be good for us." The boss's view, stated as a view.
- **E — Examples.** "At my last plant, we struck for 6 weeks." A story, even a slanted one.

What makes it illegal is the threat attached: "…and if we unionize, I can't guarantee the plant stays open." That's a threat dressed as a prediction.

## What to do now

1. When the boss talks, sort it: fact, opinion, example — or threat?
2. Write down anything that crosses the line, word for word.
3. Prepare your answers in advance. The [decoder tool](/vote/boss-campaign) sorts any message the boss sends.

## Watch out

The line moves. The current Board is expected to loosen limits on employer "predictions" (see [what's changed](/rights/whats-changed)). Assume "we might close" talk is coming, and teach your coworkers the answer before it does.
