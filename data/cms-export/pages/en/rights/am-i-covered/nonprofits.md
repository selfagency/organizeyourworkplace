**Nonprofit workers have the same federal organizing rights as everyone else. Your mission doesn't cancel your rights.**

## What this means for you

Most nonprofit employees — shelters, food banks, museums, advocacy groups, childcare centers, unions themselves — are covered by the National Labor Relations Act, just like for-profit workers. Your employer's tax status doesn't matter. The law protects nonprofit workers acting together for better pay and conditions.

## Watch out

Two limits:

- **Very small employers.** The NLRB generally won't take cases from employers with only a tiny amount of business, so a three-person nonprofit may fall outside its reach in practice.
- **Religious organizations' religious workforces.** A church can claim a religious exemption for employees who perform and spread its faith — for example, clergy and religious educators. Lay staff doing secular work (a church bookkeeper, a shelter janitor) are usually still covered. See [religious employers](/rights/am-i-covered/religious-employers).

## What to do now

1. If your employer is a nonprofit, assume you're covered — then confirm with your nearest [NLRB office](/help/nlrb-office) if the workplace is tiny or religious.
2. Everything in this site's [organizing guide](/organize) applies to you as-is.
3. For coverage edge cases, start at [am I covered?](/rights/am-i-covered).
