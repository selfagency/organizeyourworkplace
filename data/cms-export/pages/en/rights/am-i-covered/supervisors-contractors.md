**Supervisors, independent contractors, agricultural and domestic workers: the NLRA doesn't protect you. You still have routes — they're just different.**

## What this means for you

The NLRA leaves out:

- **Supervisors** — people who can hire, fire, or direct others' work as the employer's representative. (The definition is narrow; employers often stretch it to shrink unions' reach. Whether you're really a "supervisor" is a legal question.)
- **Independent contractors** — with narrow exceptions.
- **Agricultural workers** — farmhands and most field labor.
- **Domestic workers** — housekeepers, nannies, home care aides employed by households.

## Your routes

1. **Worker centers.** Community organizations that support workers outside the NLRA — wage claims, safety, direct action. [Find one →](/help/worker-centers)
2. **State and local law.** Some states extend organizing rights or wage protections the NLRA doesn't. Domestic-worker bills of rights exist in several states.
3. **Direct action.** Collective action still works without NLRA protection — it just carries more risk, because the legal backstop is weaker. Organize carefully, with a worker center or union that knows your industry.
4. **Reclassify?** Some "contractors" are really employees misclassified by the employer. A lawyer can assess that — [find one →](/help/lawyers).
