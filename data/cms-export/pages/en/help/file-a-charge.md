**You can file a charge yourself, for free, with help from NLRB staff. Here's the whole process.**

## What this means for you

A charge (Form NLRB-501) is how you ask the NLRB to investigate an employer or union that broke federal labor law [A73]. Key facts [A71]:

- **Anyone can file** — you don't have to be the person targeted.
- **Six months** from the event, by statute [A73].
- **Free.** No filing fee, no lawyer needed. Information officers will draft the charge with you [A73].

What happens after filing [A72]: the region investigates — statements, evidence, affidavits. Most charges settle, get withdrawn, or get dismissed. A decision on the merits typically comes in **7 to 14 weeks**; hard cases take longer. If the NLRB finds the law was broken, it seeks remedies: reinstatement, back pay, and more.

Since late 2025, charges filed carry a **two-week evidence deadline** from docketing: a chronological timeline, documents, and a witness list with contacts [A79]. Miss it and the charge can be dismissed.

## What to do now

1. **Call the regional office first** ([finder](/help/nlrb-office)). Tell the information officer what happened. They'll tell you what's covered and can draft the charge for you [A73].
2. **Assemble your evidence pack now** so the two-week window doesn't bite [A79]:
   - a dated timeline: who, what, when, exact words;
   - documents: write-ups, schedules, letters, messages, phone records;
   - witness list: names, phones, emails, one line on what each saw.
3. **File the charge** with the region covering your worksite [A73].
4. **Serve a copy on the employer** — the charge isn't processed until it's served, and the rules make service your responsibility [A73]. The information officer explains how.
5. **Respond to every docketing letter** — that's where the two-week clock comes from [A79].

## Watch out

Not every rotten thing your boss did is an unfair labor practice — the NLRA covers collective activity about pay and conditions, not every workplace dispute [A73]. The information officer will tell you quickly if your case fits. If it doesn't, see [lawyers](/help/lawyers) for other routes (discrimination, wage claims).
