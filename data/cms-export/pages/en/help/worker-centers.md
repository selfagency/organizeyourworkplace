**You don't always need a lawyer or a union. Worker centers and organizing coaches fill the gap — free or cheap.**

## What this means for you

A **worker center** is a community organization that supports workers outside the union/legal system: know-your-rights training, help confronting a boss, wage-theft recovery, organizing coaching. They're usually nonprofits, often focused on specific industries (food service, domestic work, day labor, farm work) or communities.

For organizing drives specifically, two free options cover the whole country:

- **[EWOC — Emergency Workplace Organizing Committee](https://workerorganizing.org/support/)**: volunteer organizer coaching for any worker, any industry [P1-8].
- **[AFL-CIO organizer referral](https://aflcio.org/contact-union-organizer)**: routes you to the union that should be organizing your sector [P1-9].

For legal-adjacent help, **[LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org/find-help/)** finds free legal aid by state [A76].

## What to do now

1. **For a union drive:** start with [EWOC](/unions/no-union-yet). Free, experienced, and they'll work at your pace.
2. **For a local worker center:** search `worker center` plus your city, or your industry plus `workers center` (like `restaurant workers center Chicago`). Then check it's real: a nonprofit with a physical space, staff, and recent activity.
3. **For wage theft or discrimination:** LawHelp.org routes you to free legal aid in your state [A76].
