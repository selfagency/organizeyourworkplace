**Union money is member money, and federal law treats it that way. Handle it openly from dollar one.**

## What this means for you

The LMRDA requires most private-sector unions to file annual financial reports — **LM-2** (large unions, full detail), **LM-3** (mid-size, less detail), **LM-4** (small, minimal) — all public in OLMS's Online Public Disclosure Room [A84]. Any member can look up what any union paid its officers, spent on lawyers, or owes.

What that means for a new local: your money records start mattering the day you collect the first dollar. Receipts, a budget, two-signature controls, and an annual report to the membership — boring habits that are also legal duties [A83].

**Dues** are set by your members, usually in the bylaws, and typically collected after the first contract wins. They pay for: strike funds, legal support, training, the union hall, and your parent body's per-capita tax.

## What to do now

1. **Don't build payment processing here.** This site collects nothing. Use standard membership software your parent union or peer locals already run — see [don't build dues collection](/run).
2. **Open a union bank account** with two-officer signatories and monthly statements shared with the executive board.
3. **Adopt a budget annually by member vote** — the bylaws should require it.
4. **Pick your LM form class correctly** and file on time; the OLMS disclosure room has the forms and instructions [A84].
5. **Publish a plain-language money summary** to members every year. Transparency builds the trust that gets dues paid.

## Watch out

Officers can't be paid for union work without membership approval in your bylaws; loans from union funds to officers are effectively prohibited under the LMRDA's fiduciary rules [A83]. When in doubt, disclose — to members and in filings.
