**The day after the contract is signed, the boss starts working on the next generation of workers. So should you.**

## What this means for you

Union density isn't a status — it's a practice that decays. Turnover in many industries tops 50% a year, which means in two years most of your shop never voted for the union. Officers get re-elected unopposed. Meetings shrink to the faithful. The boss learns your union is a paperwork machine, and acts like it.

The fix is the same muscle you built organizing: one-on-ones, mapping, committee. The targets just change — new hires, the cynical, the never-involved.

## What to do now

1. **Run new-hire orientation as a union right** if you can win it in the contract — 15 minutes with a steward and a card beats a year of hoping they hear about you.
2. **Map the shop every quarter.** Same method as the first campaign: who's in, who's out, who's new. See [map your workplace](/organize/map-your-workplace).
3. **Rotate the committee.** Every election cycle, some officers step back. Bench strength is democracy — see [officer elections](/run/officers-elections).
4. **Keep the survey alive** [A4]: response rate by department is your density gauge. Falling rate in a department = falling power.
5. **Win small things publicly.** A fixed AC, a restored shift — see [small fights first](/organize/actions). The union's story has to keep being "when we act together, we win."

## Watch out

The quiet killer is the meeting that only the officers attend. If a meeting dies two cycles in a row, change it — different time, food, childcare, a real agenda — before it dies for good.
