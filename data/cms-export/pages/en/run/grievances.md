**A contract without grievances is a wish list. The grievance procedure is where the contract gets real — or gets exposed.**

## What this means for you

A **grievance** is a formal, written complaint that the contract was violated — a bad write-up, a skipped break, a schedule change, a denial of Weingarten representation [A86]. Your contract defines the steps: usually steward → supervisor → written grievance → higher levels → arbitration.

Two clocks rule everything: the **deadline to file** each step (often 10–30 days — your contract's numbers) and the **deadline for management to answer**. Miss the filing window and even a righteous case dies. The grievance tool in the toolkit tracks these deadlines per case.

## What to do now

1. **Log every contract violation** in the grievance tracker — date, article, people, witnesses. Even ones you won't grieve. Patterns win arbitrations.
2. **File on time, every time.** If the deadline is tight, file the simple version first and amend — late beats perfect.
3. **Match the demand to the ask:** back pay, remove the write-up, restore the shift. "Fix the culture" isn't a remedy.
4. **Use grievances to organize,** not just to win: the member with the write-up has a department watching. Win it publicly and the contract's value doubles overnight.
5. **Track outcomes** — what you won, what it cost, which articles management tests most. That data feeds your next bargaining round. See [the survey](/contract/survey).

## Watch out

Don't grieve every annoyance — the procedure clogs and management stops taking it seriously. And don't settle cases quietly for individual relief when the violation hits everyone: a settlement that fixes the write-up but leaves the policy intact is half a win.
