**Bylaws are boring on purpose. They're the fence that keeps your union democratic when feelings run high.**

## What this means for you

Bylaws answer the questions that end friendships if left unwritten: Who can call a meeting? How many members make a quorum? What does the president actually control? Who touches the money? How does someone get removed?

The LMRDA requires your bylaws to be consistent with its member bill of rights — free speech and assembly, fair elections, due process in discipline, and financial transparency [A83]. Officers must be elected per your constitution and bylaws, within the law's time limits [A82].

## What to do now

1. **Start from a template, adapt hard.** Ask your international or EWOC for current bylaws of similar locals. Delete anything you don't understand — you'll be living under it.
2. **Must-haves:** membership rights and dues; officer roles and terms; meeting schedule and quorum; election procedures; grievance and discipline process (with due process); amendment procedure; how money is approved and reported.
3. **Write the removal clause seriously.** The day an officer goes bad — or just goes absent — is the wrong day to discover you have no process. Members can also remove local officers by secret-ballot vote for serious misconduct under the LMRDA's safety net [A83].
4. **Adopt bylaws by member vote** at a properly noticed meeting. Attendance and vote counts go in the minutes — keep them.
5. **File what the law requires.** Constitutions and bylaws are filed with the DOL for national unions; locals file per their parent's procedures [A84].

## Watch out

Don't copy a 40-page bylaws from a giant local — you can't enforce what you don't understand. And don't skip the amendment procedure: you WILL amend them in year two.
