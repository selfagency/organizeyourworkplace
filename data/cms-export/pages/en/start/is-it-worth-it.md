**Yes — if you and your coworkers stay in it together. Here are the real numbers and the real risks.**

## The payoff

- Union workers' median weekly pay is **$1,404 vs. $1,174** for nonunion workers.
- The union wage premium is about **12.8%** for the same job, education and experience.
- About **43% of nonunion workers** — roughly 56 million people — say they'd vote yes tomorrow.

All from the Economic Policy Institute's 2025 analysis [P1-12].

## The costs

- **Time.** Most campaigns take 6–18 months from first conversation to election. A first contract often takes another year or more.
- **Stress.** The boss will fight back. Expect one-on-one pressure, mandatory anti-union meetings, and promises that always come right before the vote.
- **Risk.** Some organizers do get fired. The law says they can't — but the law is a weak deterrent, and it moves slowly. Strength in numbers is the real protection. That's why this site teaches you to build a big majority *before* you go public.

## The odds, honestly

- About 5.9% of private-sector workers are in a union (10.0% counting everyone) [P1-12]. Most campaigns that reach an election win it. Most campaigns that *fail* fail earlier — they go public too soon, with too small a committee.
- The single best predictor of winning: **how many coworkers you've talked to one-on-one before the boss finds out.**

The law is shifting against organizers right now. Check [what's changed](/rights/whats-changed) before you rely on any legal rule.
